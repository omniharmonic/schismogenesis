An arts lab for dialogue across difference

You didn't pick your enemies. You copied them.

antimemes.is builds instruments for seeing the culture you're inside, meeting the people on the far side of it, and getting credit for understanding them. Not for winning.

Desire is borrowed.

We want what our models want. Online everyone is a model and everyone is a rival, so wants and grudges spread the same way: by contact.

Then the room splits in two.

Gregory Bateson called it schismogenesis: two groups define themselves against each other, every move answered by its mirror, until the difference is all either side has left.

Feeds run it at scale. Sort everyone by one question and the middle disappears.

And every crisis wants a victim.

When rivalry has worn away every other difference, crowds settle it the oldest way: all against one. René Girard called it the scapegoat mechanism. Online it never finishes. There is a new one every morning.

The node in the middle is nobody. That's the point.

The usual fixes feed the machine.

Every move below is a reasonable thing to do. Each one hands the rivalry a new object.

  1. Debate them

    Now there's a winner and a loser, and a highlight reel of the loss.

  2. Fact-check them

    Now there's a referee, and both sides can fight the referee.

  3. Post more good content

    More models to imitate, faster. The feed doesn't care which side you're on.

  4. Dunk on the people dunking

    Congratulations, you found today's scapegoat.

  5. Stop fighting over the same object

    Give people something to want that the other side has to grant, and that never runs out.

An antimeme is an idea you can't copy. You have to meet it.

A take spreads in a second. Having listened to someone doesn't spread at all. Understanding has to be done again, by each of us, every time. That's the opening this lab works in. Call it anti-mimesis: meeting difference without imitating it or fighting it.

Girard's diagnosis

Desire is learned from models. Rivals become each other's doubles. Crowds end the crisis by choosing a victim. We take the diagnosis and refuse the conclusion some of his readers drew, that order needs an enemy.

Habermas's wager

People can coordinate by reaching understanding, not by force or price, when what they say has to answer for itself. The lifeworld, the shared background we all talk from, is what feeds keep colonising. We build for the lifeworld.

Anti-debates, steelmen, notes across lines

In an anti-debate nobody rebuts until they can state the other side's case so well the other side agrees with it.

  1. A states B's case at its strongest.
  2. B says "yes, that's us", or corrects it.
  3. Only then does A disagree.

A clean, mutually understood disagreement counts as a result.

        How the map feeds the rest

        From the map to meta-consensus.

        1. SeeLive now

          The map places you.

          The camps come from public figures' own words. Your answers drop you among them. Say you land in the camp called one camp, the ringed marker.

        2. MeetNext

          Go to the far side.

          From there, read the strongest case of the camp farthest from you on the map, another camp. Then write a description of them that they would sign.

        3. MoveComing

          They sign it. You earn it.

          Only the described side can sign. A signed description earns bridging reputation, Community Notes style. Agents can draft on a separate board and never sign.

        4. Meta-consensus

          Every camp, described by every other.

          Not agreement. Each dashed line is a description waiting for the other side to sign. Signed so far: 0 of 12.

        Rules of engagement

        • Public figures, public words

          People are placed only from what they've published, and every placement can be disputed.

        • Nobody private

          We infer nothing about private people. Your answers stay in your browser unless you choose to share them. Privacy

        • Short quotes, always linked

          We quote a sentence or two and link to the source. Full transcripts are never republished.

        • Agents never ratify

          Software can draft and assist. Only people in the described group can sign a description of it.

        • No verdicts

          We don't score who's right. We score whether groups can describe each other.

        • Adults only

          The survey is for people 18 and over.
