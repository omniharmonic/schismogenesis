About and method

The AI Futures Fingerprint is part of Dialogos, a project by Benjamin Life. Its aim is not to sort people into teams. It is to help people who disagree about AI describe each other in terms the other side would sign.

Where the statements come from

We read 93 public works by 27 people who shape the conversation about AI and society. A language model proposed claims, each tied to an exact quote; code checked every quote against the source, and a second, blind reading had to agree before a claim counted. Claims were merged into 360 ideas.

The 24 statements are ideas that thinkers hold on both sides. Code picked them by a fixed rule: statements that split the most pairs of thinkers, spread across what AI is, what matters and what to do, with a bonus for covering thinkers we'd otherwise know little about. The same data always gives the same statements.

How you're compared

For each thinker we count the statements where you both took a side, and how many you agree on. A thinker needs at least three shared statements to be ranked, and the score is smoothed so that one of one doesn't beat nine of eleven. Your neighbourhood is the school of your nearest thinker. Schools are working labels made by clustering the thinkers' ideas.

Silence never counts as disagreement. If we found no stated position, that thinker simply isn't compared on that statement.

The live room

In the room, opinion groups are found the way Polis finds them: principal components of the votes, small base clusters, then two to five groups chosen by how cleanly they separate, checked by resampling. Bridging statements are found the way Community Notes ranks notes: a model that separates how much people agree with a statement from how much that agreement depends on which side they're on. A bridge is something people on both sides agree with.

New voices

Voices added in November 2026 are compared on the statements in the same way. Until the full atlas is rebuilt with them, each new voice is placed on the map next to the thinkers they agree with most, and is marked as a new voice.

Disclosures

  • The reading of public work was done with Claude, a model made by Anthropic. Jack Clark, a co-founder of Anthropic, is one of the voices on the map.
  • Divya Siddarth's Collective Intelligence Project has worked with Anthropic, and Anton Korinek is reported to advise Anthropic on economics.
  • Fourteen statements are shown in neutral rewordings. Two blind reviewers confirmed that each means the same as the original reading. You can see the original on each statement's page.

What this can't tell you

  • None of the 27 thinkers has reviewed how they are read here. Each is read from about three works.
  • Some thinkers have too few stated positions on these statements to compare. Your result names them.
  • Twenty-four statements and twenty-seven people show a pattern, not a population.

If you think a thinker is misread, use “Report a misreading” on your result. Every report is answered.
