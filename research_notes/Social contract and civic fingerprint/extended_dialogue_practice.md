# Extended public dialogue practice: how long-running dialogues on foundational questions work, and what a tiny team can do

Researched 2026-11-02. Scope excludes Polis and Community Notes algorithm weaknesses, which are already in `reports/Memetic mapping and computational democracy.md`.

**How to read the evidence.** Each finding carries a tag:
- [EVAL]: independent or peer-reviewed evaluation.
- [PRIMARY]: official record or the organizer's own report, used for plain facts.
- [PROMO]: the organizer's or funder's own success claim. Do not treat it as evaluation.
- [NEWS/WIKI]: journalism or Wikipedia.
- [ABSTRACT]: I saw only an abstract or search summary, not the full text.

Most pages were read through a summarizing fetch tool, so check exact numbers against the source before quoting them publicly. Where a search summary did not make clear which page held a claim, I say so. Conflicts are flagged in place.

## Case evidence: design, participation, duration, cost, drop-off, impact, and what killed the failures

### Takeaway
No case failed for lack of participation or a poor text alone. Processes died at the handoff to whoever held power (Iceland, Chile, vTaiwan, the French climate convention), or because the drafting body was seen as one side's (Chile, twice). The clearest success, Ireland, was cheap and modest in scale. It had a state mandate, a random sample, and a binding public vote at the end. Bowling Green shows an open Polis can reach about 1 in 10 local residents in 33 days, but only as paid-for input to a government-commissioned plan, and its representativeness is unevaluated.

**Iceland**
- Parliament passed the enabling act in June 2010. A random National Forum of 950 met that November, and a 25-member drafting body used its conclusions. Parties were excluded from preparation and drafting. Amending the constitution required two parliaments with a general election between them — [ConstitutionNet](https://constitutionnet.org/news/why-making-crowd-sourced-constitution-iceland-failed) [analysis].
- The Supreme Court voided the 2010 election on technical grounds. Parliament then appointed the top 25 vote-getters to a Council, which the author argues made it parliament-appointed rather than elected. The Council approved the bill 25-0 — [openDemocracy](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/democracy-on-ice-post-mortem-of-icelandic-constitution/) [partisan author who favors the bill].
- The Council had under four months, from April to late July 2011 — [Yourstory/ICON search set](https://yourstory.com/2011/06/crowdsourcing-in-iceland) [NEWS, verify page]. It took input through Facebook, Twitter, email, YouTube and Flickr. Counts: about 3,600 comments and proposals and 395 formal suggestions — [conference slides, Thorarensen](https://jura.ku.dk/pdf/arrangementer/2022/IACL_Panel2__Bj_rg_Thoransen.pdf).
- Almost 10% of written proposals generated a change in the draft, mainly on rights — [OII Policy blog, summarizing the Policy & Internet study](https://blogs.oii.ox.ac.uk/policy/tag/crowd-sourcing/feed/index.htm) [ABSTRACT].
- Landemore finds the crowdsourced text marginally but significantly better than the experts' on some crucial aspects. She also says the Council lacked the time, tools or training to process the crowd's input, and that its outsider self-image hurt its legitimacy over time — [Landemore summary](https://democraticaudit.com/2014/07/23/the-icelandic-experience-challenges-the-view-that-constitutional-process-must-be-exclusionary-and-secretive) and [ICON 2020 record](https://openpolar.no/Record/croxfordunivpr:10.1093%2Ficon%2Fmoaa004) [ABSTRACT].
- The October 2012 advisory referendum had 48.9% turnout and 66.9% backed the draft as a basis. All six questions passed. The changes were still unimplemented as of 2021 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Icelandic_constitutional_referendum) [WIKI].
- **What killed it:** 32 of 63 MPs publicly backed the bill, but it never reached a vote. The Independence Party and Progressives filibustered, and the majority would not end the filibuster. The April 2013 election brought those two parties to power. Special-interest groups ignored the open invitation to comment, and some academic critics surfaced only after the referendum — [openDemocracy](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/democracy-on-ice-post-mortem-of-icelandic-constitution/). One analysis argues that treating constitution-making as non-political and keeping it apart from the political forum was bound to fail — [ConstitutionNet](https://constitutionnet.org/news/why-making-crowd-sourced-constitution-iceland-failed).

**Ireland**
- The 2016-18 Assembly had a chair plus 99 citizens sampled to reflect gender, age, location and social class. Members got expenses and no other pay. It ran from October 2016 to April 2018 after two deadline extensions. Budget went from an estimate of €600,000, to €2 million set aside, to €2,355,557 actually spent to March 2019 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)) [WIKI]. A referendum on 25 May 2018 replaced the abortion prohibition, followed by legislation — same source.
- Selection used stratified random sampling with door-to-door calls, plus 99 substitutes — [Participedia](https://participedia.net/case/13695).
- 17 members withdrew before the first working meeting, and 11 more before the final abortion meeting. In 2018, seven replacements had to be removed because a Red C polling-firm employee recruited them through acquaintances instead of randomly — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)). Assembly adviser David Farrell has said the assemblies were imperfect, including problems with supposedly random selection — [Economist copy](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5cb97189ca525b7a4f3f5775/t/60a720e6c37bbe66f961662f/1621565677726/20200919_Economist.pdf).
- Evaluation of the later gender-equality assembly involved observing every public session, surveying members each weekend, and interviewing members and organizers — [Suiter, Park, Galligan, Farrell](https://core.ac.uk/download/482671455.pdf) [EVAL]. That is a usable evaluation template.
- Convention 2012-14: 79 of 100 members voted for same-sex marriage in the constitution in April 2013 — [ICCL / PILA](https://pila.ie/resources/bulletin/2013/04/24/constitutional-convention-votes-on-same-sex-marriage). The 2015 referendum passed 62.07% to 37.93% — [IrishCentral](https://www.irishcentral.com/culture/ireland-same-sex-marriage-referendum).

**Chile**
- 2022: 61.87% rejected on 85.84% turnout — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Chilean_constitutional_referendum). It was the first Chilean vote combining compulsory voting with automatic registration — [CEP](https://static.cepchile.cl/uploads/cepchile/2023/07/668_chuaqui-et-al.pdf); the 2020 entry plebiscite was voluntary at about 50% turnout — [search set](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/understanding-rejection-why-the-first-constitutional-process-fai/51690490) [verify page]. So the electorate that judged the text was far broader than the one that launched the process.
- Reasons offered in 2022: 59% said the convention's composition did not reflect Chile's diversity and 63% did not feel represented; skepticism about plurinationality, Indigenous justice and Congress's role — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Chilean_constitutional_referendum). Alemán and Navia cite a radical text, inexperienced members and a falling government — [via Springer](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/understanding-rejection-why-the-first-constitutional-process-fai/51690490) [ABSTRACT]. Observers disagree on how much was misinformation versus deeper causes.
- 2023: the mirror image. A 24-person expert commission wrote a preliminary draft, the right won three-fifths of the elected council, and the Republican Party alone held a veto. Rejected 55.76% on 84.48% turnout; women under 34 opposed at 70.1% — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chilean_constitutional_referendum). Critics blamed Republican "maximalism"; one pundit read it as left-wing revenge for 2022 — same source.
- Participation machinery did not save it. One source reports thousands of hearings and 78 popular initiatives at 15,000 signatures each; another, apparently on the second process, reports 31 qualified and 236,000+ participants, 70% men and 74% highly educated — [ConstitutionNet](https://constitutionnet.org/node/19135). Across both processes, over a million took part in citizen initiatives — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/A6E1873CFF8A0FA595D92830539DFA84/core-reader) [verify].

**Mongolia**
- Law passed 9 February 2017 requiring a deliberative poll before Parliament considers amendments to crucial provisions — [Equality by Lot](https://equalitybylot.com/2017/05/03/new-law-requiring-deliberative-poll-process-for-constitutional-amendment-in-mongolia/). Of 1,568 sampled, 1,515 did the first interview (96%). Of 785 invited, 669 (85%) completed the weekend. Support for a two-chamber parliament fell from 61% to 30% — [Fishkin and Zandanshatar](https://constitutionnet.org/news/deliberative-polling-constitutional-change-mongolia-unprecedented-experiment) [authors were insiders]. The constitution was amended in November 2019 — same search set.

**Taiwan**
- UberX 2015: 4,500+ on Polis voted on 145 opinions. The four stages were: define topic and recruit stakeholders; Polis; two-hour public meeting; draft bill. Join: 13,853 proposals by June 2022, 289 met the 5,000 threshold (about 2%), and agencies must reply with reasons. Bertelsmann says Join lacks deliberation, vTaiwan favors the tech-savvy and is confined to digital issues, and government can decline to engage — [Bertelsmann](https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/en/our-projects/democracy-and-participation-in-europe/shortcut-archive/shortcut-8-trailblazers-of-digital-participation-taiwans-join-platform-and-vtaiwan) [friendly source].
- Co-creator Jason Hsu: no major decisions since 2018, hard to use, not binding so "legislators don't take it seriously", limited to digital issues, and Join "made vTaiwan redundant" — [syndicated article](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/taiwan-tried-digitize-democracy-huge-084810185.html) [NEWS].
- Beth Noveck largely accepts this while crediting 200,000+ participants across 26 pieces of national legislation. She says Madrid fared worse: nearly 500,000 sign-ups, 28,000 proposals, one became policy — [Living Library](https://thelivinglib.org/?p=57365). A weak October 2025 wiki note says vTaiwan is now volunteer-driven with no government support — [Scrapbox](https://scrapbox.io/nishio/vtaiwanは2025年現在は政府支援を受けていない).

**France**
- Grand Débat: 500,000+ online participants. Most participants did not support the Yellow Vests, unlike the public then, which the authors read as strong political self-selection — [Monnery and Wolff, Public Choice](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-04197291.html) [EVAL/ABSTRACT]. The self-selected input of a national listening exercise excluded the very movement that triggered it.
- Climate Convention: 149 proposals, 3 rejected by Macron, who had pledged to pass the rest "sans filtre" — [IDDRI](https://www.iddri.org/fr/print/pdf/node/25644). A government tracker reports 100 measures implemented in whole or part and 46 in progress — [ecologie.gouv.fr](https://www.ecologie.gouv.fr/tableau-suivi-mise-en-oeuvre-des-propositions-convention-citoyenne-climat) [self-reported]. A 2026 assessment says many were modified, softened or partly applied in the 2021 law — [ASFE](https://alliancesolidaire.org/2026/04/08/la-convention-citoyenne-pour-le-climat-une-ambition-democratique-au-bilan-contraste/) [advocacy-adjacent]. Members themselves graded the government poorly — [L'info durable](https://www.linfodurable.fr/politique/la-convention-climat-note-le-gouvernement-qui-na-presque-jamais-la-moyenne-24264).

**Conference on the Future of Europe**
- 5 million+ unique platform visitors and 700,000+ event participants — [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/get-involved/engage-eu-policymaking/conference-future-europe_en) [PROMO]. Some proposals entered the 2023 Commission work programme, and a December 2022 feedback event followed — same. Total cost was not disclosed — [Politico via search set](https://europeanconservative.com/?p=6124) [verify page]. Greens/EFA complain highly endorsed platform proposals were ignored — [Greens/EFA](https://greens-efa.eu/en/article/news/conference-on-the-future-of-europe-what-now?pdf=28).

**US cases**
- America in One Room 2019: 523 sampled voters by NORC, 844 controls; statistically significant depolarization across 5 policy areas in the short term. A year later 463 of 523 were resurveyed; participants had mostly reverted on policy and feelings toward opponents. The lasting effect was more civic engagement among low-interest participants — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/America_in_One_Room) [WIKI, not checked against paper]; direction confirmed by [Hoover](https://www.hoover.org/plus/research/can-deliberative-discussions-heal-political-divides).
- America's Town Hall / AmericaSpeaks: ran 1995 to January 2014. Claimed reach conflicts: 180,000 or 130,000+ people. "Our Budget, Our Economy" (2010) had 3,000 people in 19 communities — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AmericaSpeaks), [GovLoop](https://govloop.com/community/blog/sad-news-to-share-from-americaspeaks). An organization with 19 years and 150 projects still closed.
- American Assembly: its 2018 Bowling Green Polis drew 2,000+ participants, 600+ statements and nearly 250,000 votes. Discussion centered on agreement areas such as traffic and jobs, although divisive topics arose — [American Assembly](https://piano-tuatara-5xkr.squarespace.com/publications-blog/tag/2018) [PRIMARY].
- What Could BG Be: 14 February to 17 March 2025; 7,890 people, 3,940 ideas, 1,034,868 votes — [StateScoop/Jigsaw results](https://statescoop.com/bowling-green-kentucky-ai-community-planning/), [WBKO](https://www.wbko.com/2025/04/15/results-what-could-bg-be-survey-released/). Open to anyone who lives, works or spends time in the county, about 1 in 10 residents. Over half of ideas drew 80%+ agreement. 83% of participants believed it would yield a better plan, 96% of leaders said it helped them represent the community, and the county approached federal government on riverfront funding — [Apolitical](https://apolitical.co/en/navigator/case-studies/using-ai-to-make-sense-of-nearly-8000-residents-ideas-for-a-25-year-community-plan) [PROMO]. Run by Innovation Engine with Jigsaw and the Computational Democracy Project, Google.org support, commissioned through Warren County's BG 2050 — [About the project](https://www.whatcouldbgbe.com/about-the-project). Pillar groups of 10 to 12 for each of 8 themes then write plans — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/bowling-green-ky-used-ai-to-drive-public-engagement).
- "Kentucky Polis": I found Bowling Green only, no statewide process.

**Decidim**
- Barcelona registered users tripled in four years to 102,601 — [Barcelona](https://www.barcelona.cat/infobarcelona/en/over-100000-people-take-part-in-participatory-process-in-the-current-term-of-office_1268198.html) [PRIMARY]. The 2020 budget put €30M behind top proposals and drew 64,571 participants, 11,970 in person — [Decidim](https://decidim.org/case-studies/barcelona-budgeting/) [PROMO]. A 2018 snapshot: 30,705 participants, 13,450 proposals, 9,200 accepted — [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/projects/decidim-barcelona).

### Inferences
- **The killers repeat.** (1) No path through the veto player (Iceland, vTaiwan). (2) Drafters seen as one camp (Chile 2022, Chile 2023). (3) A promise of "no filter" not kept (France). (4) Critics absent until the end (Iceland). (5) Self-selection excluding the very group at issue (Grand Débat).
- **Chile is the main warning for Dialogos.** Each drafting body won a real electoral majority of its moment and overreached. A process owned by one cluster reproduces the cleavage, and the broader public then rejects it. This supports the project's meta-consensus goal over producing a text.
- **Barcelona's 68% acceptance versus Madrid's 1 in 28,000** suggests committed institutional processing matters much more than the platform, though the two figures measure different things and are not directly comparable.
- **Polis alone is a one-month instrument.** The biggest open US case ran 33 days, not months. Extended duration has come from staged programmes, not from sustained activity on one tool.
- **A consensus-heavy result needs caution.** BG's "over half at 80%+" fits a local agenda with low-conflict ideas. The 2018 BG report likewise found discussion gravitating to agreement. Foundational questions will not produce this pattern, and should not be sold as if they would.

### Gaps
- Costs for Iceland, Chile, Mongolia, vTaiwan, Grand Débat and America in One Room were not found. The commonly quoted 5.4M euro figure for the Climate Convention and the 1.9M Grand Débat figure are unconfirmed; do not use them.
- Iceland comment counts conflict. Other pages in the same search gave 1,600 and 16,000, and I could not tie each to a source. Use 3,600 and 395 with attribution. The Hudson article should settle this.
- Platform figures for the Conference on the Future of Europe (about 50,000 active users, 6,500 events; top proposals only a few hundred endorsements) appeared in search but [Democracy Technologies](https://democracy-technologies.org/?p=3424) timed out, so they are unconfirmed.
- "Grand Débat about 2 months" and Irish Convention politician count (33) are from memory or inferred, not confirmed.
- No statewide Kentucky Polis found. A WebProNews claim of cross-partisan agreement in Bowling Green was unverified and has no numbers.
- Chile 2022 convention composition was not retrieved.

## Mechanics: recruitment, representativeness, attrition, phases, seeding, moderation, legitimacy, no mandate

### Takeaway
Random invitation yields only about 5-6% acceptance and still skews, but stratifying the result gives a defensible sample. An open process needs a measured comparison with a reference population or its claims are weak. Attrition is modest with a mandate or a short format, and steep in unpaid multi-stage settings. With no mandate, the only legitimacy on offer is credibility of process, and that credibility falls among those who already feel the output is against them.

### Cited Findings
- **Response:** average around 5-6% across assemblies in OECD data — [Mehr Demokratie](https://www.mehr-demokratie.de/en/mehr-wissen/citizens-assemblies/news/details-view/a-book-and-its-consequences), [Haselmayer 2025](https://martinhaselmayer.github.io/papers/Haselmayer_2025_Participation_in_the_Good_Council.pdf) [search summary; attribution between these not fully confirmed]. One UK mailing: 20,000 letters, 344 registrations (1.7%) — [Sortition Foundation study](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/sortitionfoundation/pages/906/attachments/original/1663663146/dii_study.pdf?1663663146). Personal outreach nearly doubled response in one trial but did not necessarily change who responds — [UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10169072/1/Putting%20Faith%20in%20Randomness.pdf) [verify page].
- **Skews persist:** Gill and Lord find assembly members not optimally representative — [dii study](https://assets.nationbuilder.com/sortitionfoundation/pages/906/attachments/original/1663663146/dii_study.pdf?1663663146). Low-education motivation is a particular problem — [Mehr Demokratie](https://www.mehr-demokratie.de/en/mehr-wissen/citizens-assemblies/news/details-view/a-book-and-its-consequences).
- **Who drops out:** Finland 2012, immigration topic: 12,000 population to 207 participants. Age and life situation predict drop-out most, and negative views on immigration and reluctance to meet opposing opinions also matter — [Karjalainen and Rapeli](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/qualqt/v49y2015i1p407-422.html) [ABSTRACT]. This is directly relevant: dropout is not neutral on the very trait the dialogue needs.
- **Willingness is not the bottleneck:** Neblo et al. find willingness to deliberate far more widespread than expected, highest among those less likely to join partisan politics — [HKS](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/publications/who-wants-deliberate-and-why) [ABSTRACT].
- **Attrition numbers:**
  - Ireland: 28 withdrawals of 99 reported over about 18 months, replaced — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)).
  - Mongolia: 85% of invited completed a weekend — [ConstitutionNet](https://constitutionnet.org/news/deliberative-polling-constitutional-change-mongolia-unprecedented-experiment).
  - Singapore three-week online: 2,006 recruited, 510 discussed (25%) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.10720) [framing not confirmed].
  - Marathon County, two-year panel: 48 of 93 attended one round; across rounds 36% attended all four and 27% none — [Let's Talk report](https://wipps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lets-Talk-Key-Findings-Elections-Dialogues-Final-Report-1.8.2025.pdf).
- **Sampling frauds happen:** Ireland's seven improperly recruited members — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)).
- **Phases and seeding:** vTaiwan began by defining the topic and recruiting stakeholders — [Bertelsmann](https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/en/our-projects/democracy-and-participation-in-europe/shortcut-archive/shortcut-8-trailblazers-of-digital-participation-taiwans-join-platform-and-vtaiwan). Its problem-definition stage ran from weeks to a year — [Living Library](https://thelivinglib.org/?p=57365). Bowling Green's eight seeded themes missed K-12 education until Sensemaker surfaced it among 12 additional subtopics — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/bowling-green-ky-used-ai-to-drive-public-engagement).
- **Moderation:** with no budget or skills for strong disagreement, Penzance used ground rules agreed in the first session — [Citizen Network](https://citizen-network.org/library/learning-from-penzance-citizens-panel.html). Marathon County used two trained moderators per dialogue with groups of about 10 — [Let's Talk report](https://wipps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lets-Talk-Key-Findings-Elections-Dialogues-Final-Report-1.8.2025.pdf). Bowling Green's AI report flags AI-written summaries and stresses a human in the loop — [Apolitical](https://apolitical.co/en/navigator/case-studies/using-ai-to-make-sense-of-nearly-8000-residents-ideas-for-a-25-year-community-plan).
- **Legitimacy with non-participants:** voters who see an Oregon CIR statement as biased against their views rate it less credible, which limits its influence, and understanding the design may help — [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/do-hostile-media-perceptions-constrain-minipublics-a-study-of-how/) [ABSTRACT]. Critics of Ireland attacked the expert panel's prior views — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)).
- **No mandate:** without decision-maker backing, a panel risks being a talking shop — [Citizen Network](https://citizen-network.org/library/learning-from-penzance-citizens-panel.html); Aberdeen warns of a publicity exercise without real outcomes — [Aberdeen report](https://committees.aberdeencity.gov.uk/documents/s148353/Citizen%20Assemblies%2030%20August%202023%20Final.pdf). vTaiwan's non-binding status undercut it — [syndicated article](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/taiwan-tried-digitize-democracy-huge-084810185.html).

### Inferences
- **A two-tier design fits a no-mandate project.** Run open Polis for breadth, and report it weighted against a reference survey. Separately recruit a small stratified panel for depth. Do not claim either represents the US.
- **Replace rather than chase retention.** Ireland, Marathon County and most long panels survive by replacing members. Rolling cohorts match a tiny team better than one fixed months-long group.
- **Publish who was missing on the front page.** A public "who is not here" gauge per cluster fits the project's honesty rules and the French lesson.
- **Do not promise a mandate.** No case gives evidence that an unmandated process gains real power. Make the deliverable something the process itself controls: a signed mutual description. Then it does not depend on the veto player.
- **Pre-commit to rules at the start.** Publish how inputs will be handled before opening. The French "sans filtre" and Conference experiences show this promise gets judged later.

### Gaps
- No primary OECD response-rate table was read; figures are from secondary summaries. No direct measure of trust among non-participants was found.
- I found no solid cost-per-participant comparison, and no experimental evidence on seed-statement effects.
- The Marathon County 27% "none" figure sits alongside text saying all 48 election participants had joined a prior dialogue. It refers to the 93-person panel, but check the footnote before quoting it.

## Small-team models: minimum viable version, volunteers, payment and reputation

### Takeaway
There is real precedent for very cheap work: a Cornish panel cost about £1,500. Marathon County's 2-year panel shows a slower-paced pattern. No precedent I found shows a volunteer team running a national multi-month dialogue with durable cross-cleavage retention, so treat any such claim as an untested bet. Reputation alone worked only for Ireland's unpaid members, who had a state mandate. Modest pay is the norm elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- **Penzance:** almost entirely volunteer, just under £1,500 against typical £25,000-60,000 quotes. Organizers said they lacked budget or skills for strong disagreements and warned of "talking shop" risk — [Citizen Network](https://citizen-network.org/library/learning-from-penzance-citizens-panel.html) [organizer account].
- **Cost ranges:** full local assemblies around £100k, mostly sortition, pay, independent design and facilitation — [CfGS or LGA briefing in search set](https://www.cfgs.org.uk/?p=9495) [verify page]. A UK council found a full assembly exceeded its £30,000 budget — [Aberdeen](https://committees.aberdeencity.gov.uk/documents/s148353/Citizen%20Assemblies%2030%20August%202023%20Final.pdf). A European guide argues for cutting to the elements purpose requires — [EuroAlter](https://euroalter.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Guide-to-citizens-assemblies-for-citizens-assemblies-2.pdf).
- **Marathon County, Let's Talk (Wisconsin):** a marketing campaign yielded 259 applications. Random selection weighted by political leaning brought this to 127, with 91 verified, then more recruiting; panel of 93 at about 30/40/30 liberal, moderate, conservative, 27% rural. Groups of about 10, balanced 3/4/3, met over two years on youth mental health, homelessness, immigration and the presidential election. A $100 gift card per participant. Trained local facilitators are a stated goal. Funded by New Pluralists — [Let's Talk report](https://wipps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lets-Talk-Key-Findings-Elections-Dialogues-Final-Report-1.8.2025.pdf) [PRIMARY; organizer's evaluation, no significance tests].
- **Ireland:** expenses only, no other payment — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)).
- **Volunteers without institution:** vTaiwan was built by g0v civic hackers, and is reportedly volunteer-only now, without major decisions since 2018 — [syndicated article](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/taiwan-tried-digitize-democracy-huge-084810185.html), [weak note](https://scrapbox.io/nishio/vtaiwanは2025年現在は政府支援を受けていない).
- **Tools cost little, operations do not:** Polis and Sensemaker are open source and the BG dataset is public — [Jigsaw tools](https://jigsaw-code.github.io/sensemaking-tools/). But BG had a county sponsor, a local firm and Google.org — [About](https://www.whatcouldbgbe.com/about-the-project). Analysis saved an estimated 28 days of work — [Apolitical](https://apolitical.co/en/navigator/case-studies/using-ai-to-make-sense-of-nearly-8000-residents-ideas-for-a-25-year-community-plan) [PROMO].

### Inferences
Minimum viable version, assuming about 3-6 months part-time and needing Benjamin's sign-off on anything outward-facing or paid:
1. **One question, one quarter.** Pick a single social-contract question rather than "the social contract" as a whole. Iceland's four-month draft shows even a focused body could not absorb broad input.
2. **Weeks 1-4, seed.** Draft statements from public-figure material with quote anchors, and have critics in each cluster review the seed set. This answers Iceland's late critics and Bowling Green's missed topic.
3. **Weeks 5-9, open Polis.** About 30 days, matching the one strong case. Post a daily "who is missing" gauge per cluster.
4. **Weeks 10-16, small rotating dialogue.** 3-4 groups of about 10, balanced like Marathon County, using self-agreed ground rules, on the top disputes. Each participant writes the other side's position, and that side signs or corrects it.
5. **Weeks 17-20, public ratification.** A second Polis-style round on those mutual descriptions, with the scorer applied. Agents may draft only and never ratify.
6. **Report.** Publish a disagreement map, with named dissent and the method and missing-group statement.

Payment: budget a small amount if the team can, because $100 per round times 30-40 people is $3,000-4,000 per round, which is above the near-zero budget and needs approval. Otherwise rely on public signed credit plus a reputation score, and treat it as an experiment. No case shows reputation alone holds cross-cleavage participants over months.

### Gaps
- I found no evaluation comparing paid and reputation-only retention, and no evidence that stipends improve diversity in this kind of setting.
- Global Assembly budget and Citizens' Convention data were not retrieved. Team sizes for vTaiwan and BG are unavailable.
- I found no Community-Notes-style reputation reward used in an extended dialogue. This remains the project's novel, untested assumption.

## Cross-cleavage recruitment: who shows up, and the gaps with the right and rural populations

### Takeaway
Bridging groups attract more liberals by default, and conservatives need deliberate quota-based recruiting and language changes. The best-documented small panel still lost share between enrollment and attendance (28% to 23%). Rural participation held at 27%, showing the gap is manageable locally with weighted selection, but evidence is thin and mostly from advocates.

### Cited Findings
- Bridging efforts often draw more liberal participants, and a Wisconsin group brought in more conservatives partly by avoiding words that signal a left-leaning organization — [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/video/wisconsin-group-draws-conservatives-into-unity-efforts-1759964148) [NEWS].
- Marathon County: conservatives were 28% of the panel and 23% of those attending, and rural 27% of both. Separate invitations were sent per political group to manage balance — [Let's Talk report](https://wipps.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lets-Talk-Key-Findings-Elections-Dialogues-Final-Report-1.8.2025.pdf).
- A small George Mason project: conservative-leaning people were less eager to discuss politics, citing lack of respect, and most liberals did not want to discuss politics with conservatives — [GMU](https://carterschool.sitemasonry.gmu.edu/news/2018-01/analyzing-political-divide-hopes-building-bridge) [student work]. The refusal runs both ways.
- Rural Urban Bridge Initiative names distrust as a key barrier and reports less partisan hostility in its counties — [Postindustrial](https://postindustrial.com/stories/voices-trust-and-community-engagement-are-the-way-forward/) [PROMO].
- **Braver Angels:** a multi-university randomized trial on students found reductions on explicit, implicit and behavioral measures — [Brown](https://watson.brown.edu/research/2021/can-americans-depolarize-new-research-shows-initiative-draws-couples-therapy-can) [EVAL, student sample, not a general population]. A documentary about a workshop reduced polarization, but raised interest not investment in depolarizing activities — [Braver Angels library](https://braverangels.org/library/resources/can-americans-depolarize-assessing-the-effects-of-reciprocal-group-reflection-on-partisan-polarization/) [verify which paper]. Membership grew from 2,950 to 9,262 and nearly 70% report a positive view of the other side — [BA report](https://braverangels.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/BA-2020-2021-Report.pdf) [PROMO].
- **More in Common:** the "Exhausted Majority" is about two-thirds of Americans, and the most politically engaged overestimate how extreme opponents are — [Politics and Religion interview](https://www.politicsandreligion.us/the-exhausted-majority-jason-mangone-of-more-in-common-on-hidden-tribes-the-perception-gap-and-wh/), [Daily Signal interview](https://www.dailysignal.com/2019/08/12/have-you-talked-to-a-liberal-lately-you-might-have-more-in-common-than-you-think/) [organization's own research, via interviews].
- The Grand Débat skew shows a mirror effect where the aggrieved group did not come — [Monnery and Wolff](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-04197291.html).

### Inferences
- **Recruit by cluster, not by the general public.** Send separate invitations through trusted local channels per cluster, as Marathon County did, and hold quotas open. Public-figure landmarks could be the trusted door, but only through consented invitation and Benjamin's sign-off.
- **Vocabulary is a recruiting variable.** "Social contract" may itself read as a left-coded academic frame. Test alternate wordings in each cluster before launch.
- **Expect one side to arrive late or thin.** Make it a stop rule: no public synthesis until each cluster passes a floor.
- **Target the middle as well as the poles.** Neblo and More in Common both suggest the less-engaged are the under-tapped population, and One Room's only lasting gain appeared among them.

### Gaps
- I found no Braver Angels red/blue membership ratio, nor quantified data for Living Room Conversations, Civic Health Project or Listen First. No large representative survey of Republican versus Democratic willingness to join dialogues was found.

## Legible disagreement as the goal: precedent and how outcomes are reported

### Takeaway
Yes, with precedent in pieces, though not at scale. The Boston abortion talks are the moral precedent, Deliberative Mapping the formal one, and Oregon's CIR the reporting one. Meta-consensus is an established outcome in academic theory, but I found no large public process that reported it as the headline.

### Cited Findings
- **Boston talks:** after the 1994 Brookline clinic shootings, three abortion-rights and three anti-abortion leaders met confidentially for six years. Coverage says no minds changed on abortion, rhetoric softened somewhat, and relationships lasted — [CS Monitor](https://m.csmonitor.com/Daily/2022/20220801/Lessons-of-Boston-s-secret-abortion-talks-Understanding-vs.-agreement), [Essential Partners](https://whatisessential.org/history) [PROMO], [Deseret](https://www.deseret.com/faith/2025/09/23/abortion-debate-essential-partners-what-we-can-learn). Its output was a joint public statement, a 2001 op-ed. It was private, tiny and leader-level.
- **Deliberative Mapping (Burgess, Stirling et al. 2007):** treats specialists and citizens symmetrically and records both consensual judgments and divergent views — [UEA](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/25082/) [ABSTRACT].
- **Meta-consensus:** Niemeyer and Dryzek argue deliberation should aim at meta-consensus and inter-subjective rationality, and show how to measure them — [ANU](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/the-ends-of-deliberation-meta-consensus-and-inter-subjective-rati/) [ABSTRACT].
- **Oregon CIR:** the statement has key findings plus separate statements for and against, and majority and minority camps each write — [Equality by Lot](https://equalitybylot.com/2011/07/27/citizen-juries-institutionalized-in-oregon/), [Fox and Hounds](https://www.foxandhoundsdaily.com/?p=13398) [opinion]. A Swiss pilot suggests that showing the panel's internal vote changes how readers respond — [likely JDD](https://delibdemjournal.org/article/982/galley/4924/view) [verify page].
- **Disagreement reporting in sample outputs:** Sensemaker ranks ideas by agreement, disagreement or mixed opinion — [Jigsaw](https://jigsaw-code.github.io/sensemaking-tools/), [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/bowling-green-kentucky-ai-community-planning/).

### Inferences
- **Report in three layers:** (1) shared ground; (2) mapped disputes with the crux; and (3) mutually signed descriptions, each with a "signed by, disputed by" tag. Add a CIR-style per-side statement and show vote counts.
- **Headline metric:** the share of cluster pairs where side B signs side A's description of B. This extends Niemeyer and Dryzek rather than borrowing a survey shift, and America in One Room's reversion suggests opinion change is a fragile headline anyway.
- **Use the Boston talks as narrative, not as evidence.** They show a private dialogue can work but do not prove public scale.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical agonistic case that reports outcomes, and no worked "dissensus" public process. Consensus-conference dissent practice was not retrieved. Deliberative Mapping full text was not read.

## Combining phases: open idea generation, Polis-style reaction, deliberation, drafting, ratification

### Takeaway
Several sequences exist and the strongest are Ireland's (sortition, then parliament, then referendum) and vTaiwan's (define, Polis, live meeting, draft). I found no controlled study comparing sequences. Sequencing has only observational support, and Pol.is feeding a mini-public has no evaluation I could find.

### Cited Findings
- **Ireland:** assembly, then legislature, then binding referendum. Abortion 2016-18; marriage 2013 to 2015 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens%27_Assembly_(Ireland)), [IrishCentral](https://www.irishcentral.com/culture/ireland-same-sex-marriage-referendum).
- **Iceland:** random forum, then elected drafters, then open comment, then advisory referendum, then parliament. It failed only at the last link — [ConstitutionNet](https://constitutionnet.org/news/why-making-crowd-sourced-constitution-iceland-failed).
- **vTaiwan:** four-stage sequence — [Bertelsmann](https://www.bertelsmann-stiftung.de/en/our-projects/democracy-and-participation-in-europe/shortcut-archive/shortcut-8-trailblazers-of-digital-participation-taiwans-join-platform-and-vtaiwan).
- **Mongolia:** deliberative screening before parliamentary action — [Equality by Lot](https://equalitybylot.com/2017/05/03/new-law-requiring-deliberative-poll-process-for-constitutional-amendment-in-mongolia/).
- **Bowling Green:** Polis, then AI synthesis, then human pillar groups of 10-12, then officials — [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/artificial-intelligence/bowling-green-ky-used-ai-to-drive-public-engagement).
- **Conference on the Future of Europe:** platform, events and panels combined at high scale, with criticism that platform ideas were ignored — [Greens/EFA](https://greens-efa.eu/en/article/news/conference-on-the-future-of-europe-what-now?pdf=28).
- **Design research:** a comparison of two public-authority cases found a strictly deliberative design counterproductive and a hybrid path better — [Ravazzi, Unito](https://iris.unito.it/handle/2318/150585) [ABSTRACT]. Researchers split on opening mini-publics to the wider public: it can spread contestation but risks legitimacy and pressure on members — [likely Politics and Governance](https://www.cogitatiopress.com/politicsandgovernance/article/download/12057/5318) [verify page]. "Deliberate, then vote" is widely accepted, but foregrounding voting can narrow framing — [EPSR 2025, likely](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/F19135A60561D8925735B44C236E673A/S1755773925000050a.pdf/deliberative-reason-and-the-effect-of-minipublic-configurations.pdf) [verify page].

### Inferences
- **The rule of thumb is breadth, depth, breadth.** Open idea collection and Polis, small deliberation, then return to the wide public for the verdict. All the strongest sequences close with a broad signal, and the Chile cases show that a broad public will judge the result anyway.
- **The final phase should be ratification of descriptions, not of a text.** A draft "social contract" invites Chile's failure. A description each side signs avoids a win-lose vote and fits "Agents never ratify."
- **Each phase needs a named, visible handoff rule.** Iceland, Europe and France all broke at handoff.
- **A sequence longer than about 6 months needs built-in rest and re-entry.** No studied case supports continuous months of engagement. Staged seasons with defined starts are a safer design, inferred from attrition figures.
- **Cases share a limit worth telling Benjamin plainly.** With no mandate, the process cannot replicate Ireland. Its nearest honest model is Marathon County's method at small scale, Bowling Green's tooling, and Boston's aim.

### Gaps
- No controlled trial or solid evidence on sequence order was found. Searches on Polis-to-mini-public hybrids, Aufbruch, Ostbelgien and Bloom returned nothing usable; this is a good target for one follow-up search.
- I found no evidence on agents or public-figure voices taking part in an extended dialogue in these cases. AI's documented role here is summarization only (Sensemaker).
