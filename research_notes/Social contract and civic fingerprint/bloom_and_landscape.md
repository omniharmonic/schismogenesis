## 1. Which Bloom is this, and what does it do?

### Takeaway
It is the BLOOM Project (bloom-project.org), a nonprofit building a "Public Assembly on AI" of community, state and national assemblies, with Audrey Tang as senior adviser. Its assemblies are made up of humans. AI appears only as tooling, and Tang publicly rejects simulated people as a substitute for participants.

### Cited Findings
**Identity and people**
- Lawfare describes Tang as Taiwan's cyber ambassador and a senior adviser to Bloom, and describes Bloom as a nonprofit using tools, some of them AI, to reduce barriers to community engagement. [V2] — [Lawfare, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/citizens--assemblies-in-ai-governance--an-interview-with-audrey-tang)
- BLOOM's own press list calls Tang "BLOOM senior advisor" and says it "helped organize" the Utah assembly. [V] — [BLOOM site code, latestPost chunk](https://bloom-project.org/)
- Rahmin Sarabi is named co-executive director. The other co-executive director is not named there. [V2] — [Bzdek, Be The People, 31 Oct 2026](https://vincebzdek.substack.com/p/the-peacemaking-machine)
- Clara Long is credited on BLOOM's newsletter, and BLOOM's press list calls her "BLOOM's Clara Long" without a title. Her formal role is unconfirmed. [V] — [BLOOM Substack, 30 Oct 2026](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/whos-holding-the-steering-wheel); [Public Assembly announcement, 16 Oct 2026](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/introducing-the-public-assembly-on)
- Long and Richard Wilson of the Iswe Foundation co-wrote a Guardian piece arguing that democratizing AI takes citizens' assemblies with real power. [V for BLOOM's description; I did not open the Guardian text] — [BLOOM site code](https://bloom-project.org/), pointing to [Guardian, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/14/ai-overlords-democratise-technology)

**Method**
- Stated premise: "give people a chance to learn, listen, work through options and decide together what happens next." Local partners convene people across differences. [V] — [bloom-project.org](https://bloom-project.org/)
- Three tiers: up to 20 civic hosts, community assemblies February to July 2027, then state assemblies, then a national civic assembly planned for 2028. Hosts are told by 15 January 2027. [V] — [Public Assembly announcement](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/introducing-the-public-assembly-on)
- Host applications close 20 November 2026, with an information session on 10 November. [V] — [BLOOM Substack](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/whos-holding-the-steering-wheel)
- Two host tracks: a "short arc" at $10,000 to $30,000 over 3 months, and a "long arc" at $50,000 to $60,000 over 6 months that ends in a "public ratification vote." [V] — [bloom-project.org/cohort](https://bloom-project.org/cohort/)
- Each host must identify a decision-maker and a timeline, such as a county commission vote or a state bill. [V] — [bloom-project.org/cohort](https://bloom-project.org/cohort/)

**Tooling: CivicOS, described as BLOOM's open-source platform** [V] — [bloom-project.org/cohort](https://bloom-project.org/cohort/)
- Table Themer captures what each table says and shows themes emerging across the room.
- Open Poll lets residents add statements and react (agree, disagree, unsure), mapping agreement, division and common ground. A national poll exists at [all.bloomproject.us](https://all.bloomproject.us/landing).
- Public reports turn input into a readable report, for example [Central Oregon](https://report.bloomproject.us/central-oregon-ai/).

**AI participants or AI-mediated humans?**
- Tang's stated line: AI is complementary when it strengthens a muscle and competitive when it replaces one. Queues, timekeeping and transcripts can be automated. First-pass clustering is fine if participants can see and reject the categories. An AI-drafted bridging statement is fine if people then rate it. AI should not draft positions, decide which disagreements matter, or turn summaries into decisions. [V2] — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/citizens--assemblies-in-ai-governance--an-interview-with-audrey-tang)
- Tang also said "Simulated personas are not a remedy for people who were never invited." [V2] — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/citizens--assemblies-in-ai-governance--an-interview-with-audrey-tang)
- BLOOM's materials describe assemblies of citizens only. I found no claim of AI members. [V] — [BLOOM Substack](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/whos-holding-the-steering-wheel)

**Outputs**
- Utah Solutions Forum, with Utah Common Ground: 38 delegates, two days, 10 recommendations, each at 82% or higher. [V] — [Public Assembly announcement](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/introducing-the-public-assembly-on)
- The recommendations and vote shares (82% to 97%) cover an AI information hub, library AI literacy, local votes on hyperscale data centers, independent impact analyses, recusal rules, limits on nondisclosure agreements, and a consumer complaint initiative. [V] — [BLOOM site code, utahRecommendations chunk](https://bloom-project.org/)
- Mormon Women for Ethical Government is named as a Utah pilot partner and the Central Oregon Civic Action Project as the Oregon partner. [V] — [Public Assembly announcement](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/introducing-the-public-assembly-on)
- Central Oregon Civic Assembly on AI meets 5 to 6 December 2026. [V] — [BLOOM Substack](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/whos-holding-the-steering-wheel)
- Near-term public events: 6 November panel with Data & Society at the New York Public Library. [V] — [BLOOM Substack](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/whos-holding-the-steering-wheel)

**Sources disagree**
- Delegate count: BLOOM says 38; Lawfare says 40 demographically representative residents from three counties. — [BLOOM](https://bloomproject.substack.com/p/introducing-the-public-assembly-on); [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/citizens--assemblies-in-ai-governance--an-interview-with-audrey-tang)

### Inferences
- Open Poll behaves like Polis (participant statements, agree/disagree votes, an opinion map). BLOOM does not say it is Polis or built on it. A search summary calling Pol.is "BLOOM's tool" could not be confirmed, and Lawfare ties pol.is to Tang's record rather than to BLOOM's stack.
- Benjamin's phrase "AI citizen assemblies" is best read as "citizens' assemblies about AI, helped by AI tools."
- BLOOM's hard requirement of a nameable decision-maker is its strongest design feature, and it marks where Dialogos is weaker, since Dialogos currently has no decision it feeds.

### Gaps

- BLOOM calls CivicOS open source, but I could not find a code repository. A GitHub search returned nothing, so availability is unverified.
- Which model, if any, powers Table Themer and the reports is not stated. I found no LLM vendor named in the site code.

## 2. Which Declaration of Interdependence is the related one?

### Takeaway
At least six efforts use the name. The relevant one is **Declare Interdependence / We the Interdependent**, a live public-writing process stewarded by Amelia Rose Barlow, which sits alongside a January 2026 Davos working declaration she partnered on. Neither has a verified final text or a signer list in the usual sense.

### Cited Findings
**The related effort**
- We the Interdependent invites people to write a personal declaration, publicly shared and woven into a collective declaration. The page listed 47 submissions when fetched, two from groups. It states no organizer, method or funder. [V] — [wetheinterdependent.com](https://wetheinterdependent.com/declare-interdependence)
- Barlow's site describes "We the Interdependent Declare Interdependence!" as a collaborative initiative exploring how interdependence could guide political, economic and social systems. [V] — [ameliarosebarlow.com](https://www.ameliarosebarlow.com/)

That copy mentions neither an open-source model nor BLOOM. [V2] — [Yahoo syndication, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/father-gave-internet-declaration-independence-130000121.html); [Rolling Stone original, not directly readable](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-commentary/amelia-rose-john-perry-barlow-internet-declaration-1235645124/)

## 6. What gap could Dialogos fill, and who is a useful first conversation?

### Takeaway
The clearest fit is as a **measurement and bridging layer between communities** rather than as another assembly. BLOOM maps opinion within a place, but nobody I found measures whether groups can describe each other in terms the other side would sign. Everything below is my analysis, not something these groups have asked for. Contacting anyone is Benjamin's decision.

### Cited Findings
- BLOOM's Open Poll maps agreement inside a community, and hosts carry conclusions to local decision-makers. [V] — [bloom-project.org/cohort](https://bloom-project.org/cohort/)
- The Citizens' Track names a federated database of assembly outcomes as a proposed need. [V] — [report](https://citizens-track.org/2026/report/)
- Tang requires that clustering be visible and rejectable and that bridging text be rated by people. [V2] — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/citizens--assemblies-in-ai-governance--an-interview-with-audrey-tang)
- House of the People stratifies on initial attitudes to technology. [V] — [press release](https://www.houseofthepeople.uk/press-release-worlds-first-grassroots-citizens-assembly-on-ai-launches)
- Dialogos rules: agents never ratify, public figures are placed only from public output with a dispute affordance, no inference about private individuals who have not opted in, and anything outward-facing needs Benjamin's approval. [V] — `/Users/benjaminlife/dev/schismogenesis/CLAUDE.md`

**Concrete cautions**
- The Dialogos map is built from public figures, not residents. It must not be used to pick delegates or to stand for a town until validated against real populations. Recruitment is, as Tang says, political.
