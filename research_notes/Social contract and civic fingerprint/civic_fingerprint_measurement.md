## Prior instruments: dimensions, item count, validity, weakness

### Takeaway
No prior instrument does what Benjamin wants. Validated scales measure a few deep traits with many items. Public quizzes cover many issues but have little published validation. Pew is the closest structural model (30 items, nine clusters), and voting advice application (VAA) research shows that the choice of items itself changes the result.

### Inferences
- Trait batteries (MFQ-2, PVQ-RR) are stable because they measure abstractions over many items each, at about 6 and 3 items per construct. A fingerprint of contested propositions will age faster than these.
- The VAA finding is the key design warning: whoever picks the items draws the map. The Dialogos analogue is that item selection needs its own audit trail and governance.
- Bakker et al. means no self-report is a clean "pre-political" root. Moral or personality items do not escape partisanship.

- Unverified recollection: Wahl-O-Mat uses 38 theses with a double-weight option.
- Pew's Appendix B, which holds the cluster-selection details, was not read.

## How many dimensions does public opinion have, and are most people structured?

### Takeaway
Most people are partly structured, not unstructured. The best single decomposition says one left-right axis explains about 1/7 of issue opinion, real personal idiosyncrasy about 3/7, and noise about 3/7. That favours Benjamin's idea, provided the fingerprint keeps the idiosyncratic part and does not collapse people onto axes.

### Cited Findings
- One dimension explains about 1/7 of opinion variation, idiosyncratic preferences about 3/7 and response instability about 3/7, across high-profile US issues — [Lauderdale, Hanretty & Vivyan](https://chrishanretty.co.uk/pubs/10.1086_695673/index.html).
- Allowing a second dimension about doubles the share attributable to ideology. Among high-knowledge respondents, one dimension explains almost twice as much and instability is substantially lower — [same](https://chrishanretty.co.uk/pubs/10.1086_695673/index.html).
- Fowler et al. classify about 72.8% of respondents as Downsian (ideological), 20.7% Conversian (idiosyncratic) and 6.5% inattentive, using 2016 CCES data — [authors' LSE post](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/usappblog/2022/10/19/most-americans-are-ideologically-moderate). Most moderates are genuine centrists — [APSR](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/71A6A9BD7EC7A5C94F975703417F866F/S0003055422000818a.pdf/div-class-title-moderates-div.pdf).
- A published response argues this method assumes the liberal-conservative model for those it calls ideologues — [APSR response](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4C8917D91FCFD7ECA74E5D2DB5C741FE/S0003055424001333a.pdf/moderates.pdf). I could not open it or confirm its authors.
- Kinder and Kalmoe argue Americans are largely ideologically innocent; a review of their book reports moderates' policy views correlate weakly with each other — [review](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/abs/neither-liberal-nor-conservative-ideological-innocence-in-the-american-public-by-donald-r-kinder-and-nathan-p-kalmoe-chicago-university-of-chicago-press-2017-224p-7800-cloth-2600-paper/D45AB305F3BE226EB8BCA8325E5951B7).
- Ansolabehere, Rodden and Snyder attribute apparent incoherence to measurement error in single items. Averaging many items in a domain reveals well-structured, stable preferences, with stability approaching party ID as items increase — [APSR 2008](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/strength-of-issues-using-multiple-measures-to-gauge-preference-stability-ideological-constraint-and-issue-voting/16F6AF97F7B71AA0112EC9ADF78B553A).
- Ahler and Broockman's "Delegate Paradox": constituents with idiosyncratic views may be best represented by politicians who look polarized. In their example 60% are liberal on every issue, but a different 60% each time — [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/view/700783/?sc=dwhn) and [Stanford working paper record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ecl:stabus:3517).
- Baldassarri and Goldberg find three groups: ideologues, alternatives (morally conservative and economically liberal, or the reverse) and agnostics (weak links among beliefs) — [AJS, via PDF](https://typeset.io/pdf/neither-ideologues-nor-agnostics-alternative-voters-belief-4bm2749t1h.pdf).
- Hare et al. show people use the left-right scale differently, and that this DIF hides polarization in self-placement — [AJPS](https://ajps.org/2014/09/18/using-bayesian-aldrich-mckelvey-scaling-to-study-citizens-ideological-preferences-and-perceptions/).
- Traditional IRT can return latent dimensions that miss the extremes of a two-dimensional economic and social space — [APSR 2024](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/395DA1DFE3DCD7B866DC053D7554A30B/S000305542400039Xa.pdf/measurement_that_matches_theory_theorydriven_identification_in_item_response_theory_models.pdf).

### Inferences
- The disputed numbers (1/7 ideology vs 72.8% ideologues) measure different things and need not conflict. One is variance in answers, the other a person-level label.
- Landmarks are high-sophistication speakers. Sophisticated people are more constrained, so axes learned from elites will fit the public worse. This is a testable risk for the mapping step, not an established finding about Dialogos.
- For the roughly 20 to 30% who are not one-dimensional, a fingerprint is meaningful only as an item profile, not a point on axes. It should report "low fit" as an honest result, not force a label.
- Inattentive respondents are the group for whom a fingerprint is not meaningful. They need detection, not placement.

### Gaps
- No total dimension count for the US public was found. Sources support "at least two"; beyond that it depends on the item pool. Treat dimensionality as an estimate for each item bank.
- Exact values for two-dimensional and high-knowledge shares in Lauderdale et al. were not available.

## Design methods: IRT, adaptive testing, selection and forced choice

### Takeaway
Use multidimensional IRT with adaptive selection, built on a calibrated item bank, for a likely saving of about half the questions. The evidence for that saving in politics is thin: the published cases are few and direct figures were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Montgomery and Cutler present CAT as a way to minimize questions while preserving precision. They evaluated it with a survey experiment against static political knowledge scales; reduced batteries lose precision and add bias, especially at the extremes — [Political Analysis 2013](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:polals:v:21:y:2013:i:02:p:172-192_01).
- An adaptive multidimensional IRT design for VAAs (graded response model, maximum information) beat a static order of equal length at finding the best match when few items had been answered — [Sigfrid 2024](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11135-024-01845-6).
- General CAT work reportedly reaches similar true-score correlations at about half the length of non-adaptive tests, a figure quoted in a thesis and not specific to politics — [Kent](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/93137).
- Crowdsourced Adaptive Surveys: LLMs turn open-ended answers into items; embeddings remove near-duplicates (cutoff .90); Gaussian Thompson sampling picks items; a floor of .01 keeps items alive, with inverse-probability weighting. N=820 and N=319, on convenience samples, explicitly exploratory — [Velez](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12986v2).
- Thurstonian IRT removes the problems of ipsative (forced-choice) scoring — [Brown & Maydeu-Olivares](https://www.StatModel.com/download/irt%20of%20forced%20choice%20questionnaires%20epm11.pdf). Forced-choice CAT has been tried with participants against optimal static tests, though few such studies exist — [Lin et al.](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/93137), [BRM 2024](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13428-022-02037-6.pdf).
- Bayesian adaptive methods cover mixed pools of dichotomous and polytomous items — [Ren et al.](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s41237-020-00114-8.pdf).

### Inferences
- Adaptive selection and a shared map pull against each other: adaptive designs give people different items, and that makes side-by-side profile comparison weaker. A fixed anchor set plus adaptive items solves this.
- Adaptive selection optimizes precision on the dimensions you already specified. It will not reveal an unexpected axis, so reserve some random items in every session to keep exposure balanced and to allow recalibration (D-optimal design for the calibration stage is a reasonable candidate, but I found no political application).
- Velez's bank-growing design fits Dialogos's proposition bank well, with transcript propositions as the seed and participant items added under the same gates.
- Conjoint and MaxDiff suit priorities and trade-offs rather than placement, so use them for a small trade-off module.

### Gaps
- Montgomery and Cutler's exact item counts and error values, Sigfrid's accuracy curves and any item-count result for political multidimensional CAT could not be retrieved. Full texts were blocked.
- No direct evidence found for D-optimal item choice, MaxDiff or conjoint in civic typing.

## Response scale: what three options lose, and how to separate "no position" states

### Takeaway
Agree, disagree and pass is defensible for coverage but lossy, because a single "pass" merges at least five different states. Do not fix this with a "no opinion" filter, which appears to lose real views. Offer named options, and treat them as data.

### Cited Findings
- Omitting no-opinion options did not harm attitude-report quality (over-time consistency, responsiveness), and these options may suppress real opinions — [Krosnick et al. 2002](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-impact-of-no-opinion-response-options-on-data-quality-non-att).
- Newer online-panel work reports that respondents offered DK are more confident in their answers, with effects varying by issue, and that the satisficing pattern came from older face-to-face and phone data — [Elkjær & Wlezien 2024](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/77F4AFF4FCF5D2E547C85B17FE3E2A58/S2049847024000426a.pdf/estimating_public_opinion_from_surveys_the_impact_of_including_a_dont_know_response_option_in_policy_preference_questions.pdf).
- Reliability rises with response options and then plateaus — [Sun, Schmidt & Henry 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02846). Other studies put the useful range at 4 to 7 categories, with quality falling below four and little gain beyond seven (Lozano et al., via [summary](https://smartabase.com/?p=3009); check against the original).
- A five-point scale gives little disadvantage against seven under IRT — [Aybek & Toraman](https://gcris.pau.edu.tr/entities/publication/29126455-ff2f-42fb-8f4c-30bb1ed7c76c).
- Feldman and Zaller hold that people usually have considerations on both sides, and Zaller treats an answer as a sample from those considerations — [Gainous via ARP](https://journals.shareok.org/arp/article/view/273).
- Alvarez and Brehm distinguish uncertainty, which information can resolve, from ambivalence, which comes from conflict between core values — [Hard Choices](https://www.everand.com/book/530235140/Hard-Choices-Easy-Answers-Values-Information-and-American-Public-Opinion). Measures of ambivalence have lagged theory — [Gainous](https://studysites.sagepub.com/jimenezstudy/articles/Gainous.pdf).
- The ambivalence-indifference problem is old: Kaplan (1972) proposed splitting a scale into separate positive and negative parts — cited in [Gainous via ARP](https://journals.shareok.org/arp/article/view/273).
- Partisan gaps on factual items shrank about 55% with incentives for correct answers — [Bullock et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19080.pdf). A synthesis finds evidence of insincere expressive responding limited and ambiguous — [Malka & Adelman](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/perspectives-on-politics/article/expressive-survey-responding-a-closer-look-at-the-evidence-and-its-implications-for-american-democracy/6A103C2A09C4F37C5AEA5F95A7E869DD).

### Inferences
- Three options fall below the 4-to-7 range, so expect meaningful per-item loss. More items can buy that back, and three-tap answering is much faster. This is a real trade, and it should be tested and not assumed.
- Recommended response set: **Agree / Disagree**, with a secondary tap for strength, then four named non-positions:
  1. **Torn** (see both sides): ambivalence, a real position.
  2. **Don't care** (not important to me): indifference.
  3. **Don't know enough**: uncertainty, resolvable.
  4. **Wrong question** (I reject how this is framed): framing refusal.
  A plain **Skip** stays available and counts as missing data.
- "Wrong question" is the most Dialogos-native signal. Frame rejection may itself mark a cluster, and clustered refusals flag an item that encodes one camp's assumptions. No source tests this; it is a hypothesis.
- The named options must be modeled as separate categories (nominal response or mixture), not as a midpoint. Treating them as neutral would falsely place ambivalent and indifferent people at the center, which is the error Fowler et al. warn about.
- Keep factual-belief items and value items in separate modules, because they are affected differently by partisan responding.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head of 3-point vs 5-point information for political IRT items was found. Polis-specific handling of pass could not be retrieved in this session, because searches returned noise.
- Thompson-Zanna-Griffin ambivalence formula and Skitka's moral conviction work were not retrieved. Moral conviction is a likely fifth state worth checking (unverified).
- The four-way split is untested as a set.

## Stability, wording, and group differences: how long does a fingerprint hold?

### Takeaway
Single answers are noisy, multi-item domain scores are fairly stable, and trait-like scores hold for at least months. No published shelf life exists for a fingerprint, so one must be measured. A reasonable working assumption is yearly retesting of the full profile, with items re-dated more often.

### Cited Findings
- Early panel correlations for one item were low, and two-year gaps looked nearly as low as four-year gaps — [Duke course notes on Converse](https://polsci240-site-7ef0ae.pages.oit.duke.edu/lectures/04_attitudes_non-attitudes_constraint.html), [BJPS on SRC panel](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-political-science/article/src-panel-data-and-mass-political-attitudes/615DFF55F72B9576C42CE7B73D11EE58). That flat pattern is what stable views plus noise would produce (my reading, consistent with Achen).
- Achen (1975) showed measurement error is sufficient to explain low item correlations and low constraint — summarized in [Ansolabehere et al.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/strength-of-issues-using-multiple-measures-to-gauge-preference-stability-ideological-constraint-and-issue-voting/16F6AF97F7B71AA0112EC9ADF78B553A).
- A multi-trait multi-method analysis of panels from the 1970s, 1990s and 2010s found a surprising degree of stability across many policy domains — [2015 paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1532673X15607299). The excerpt names Research & Politics, but the DOI prefix indicates American Politics Research, so verify the venue.
- Some recent modeling still finds issue attitudes unstable and driven by transitory shocks — [Political Analysis listing](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/194C1204B539C7227B2EC4030C9A20C8); attribution from search excerpt, verify.
- MFQ-2 retest over seven months is reported as excellent, but values were not retrieved — [validation](https://www.aminer.org/pub/64b800d53fda6d7f069d10d2/moral-foundations-theory-validation-and-replication-of-the-mfq).
- Modest average stability can coexist with high stability on a person's own issues — [Idiosyncratic Issue Opinion](https://academic.oup.com/book/61739/chapter/542463444). Issue publics exist but are smaller and more particular than assumed — [Ryan & Ehlinger](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/abs/issue-publics/6857631A2EE6D560EF3F3324C05757F1).
- Non-equivalence undermines group comparison; the core review covers prevention and repair — [Davidov et al.](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/assessment-of-measurement-equivalence-with-cross-national-and-lon/). Alignment optimization suits very heterogeneous data — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpos.2021.642283). Sum-score comparisons can mislead without invariance — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2Fs11577-019-00598-7.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Back-of-envelope reliability (mine).** Applying Spearman-Brown to the Lauderdale shares, treating every non-ideology share as noise for an axis score:
  - One axis (item share about 1/7): 10 items ≈ .63, 20 ≈ .77, 40 ≈ .87, 80 ≈ .93.
  - Two axes (about 2/7 per item on its own axis, a generous assumption): 5 items ≈ .67, 10 ≈ .80, 20 ≈ .89, 40 ≈ .94.
  - The result: about 10 well-chosen items per axis is a realistic floor for reporting a person's score at about .8, under optimistic assumptions. A three-option format would likely lower this. These are illustrations, not estimates for any real bank.
- **Implied single-item retest (mine).** If only the 3/7 instability share is noise, one item's short-term repeatability is about .57. This suggests re-asking 3 to 5 items in every session gives each person a live error estimate.
- Dialogos needs DIF testing across clusters, not only demographics. The specific danger is an item that means different things to different clusters, which would make the map's separation partly a wording artifact. It should also be a primary test, because it is the measurement counterpart of meta-consensus (each side must read the item in the same way).
- Acquiescence: balance each axis with opposite-keyed items.
- Current-event propositions have short lifespans, so give each item a `valid_from` date and a decay rule. Separate slow layers (values, worldview) from fast ones (policy, events).

### Gaps
- No retrieved numeric coefficients for item vs scale stability (Ansolabehere's tables), for Big Five or Schwartz multi-year stability, or for any typology's class-membership stability.
- King's anchoring vignettes, acquiescence correction and US racial or educational DIF on ideology items were not retrieved.
- No study of cross-cluster DIF for memetic or online clusters was found.

## Adding intensity or importance

### Takeaway
Yes, add it, but as a separate lightweight layer. Importance appears to identify the issues a person holds firmly. Quadratic voting surveys measure intensity better than Likert, at a cost in effort, so they suit a small trade-off module, not every item.

### Cited Findings
- Attitude importance is linked to stability (Krosnick 1988, 1990, as cited) — [Barber & Pope note](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/556415944D298A98E9BE01C22800423C/S2049847023000286a.pdf/does_issue_importance_attenuate_partisan_cuetaking.pdf).
- Cavaillé, Chen and Van der Straeten: judged by behavior in simplified real-world tasks, Quadratic Voting for Survey Research separates intense from weak preferences more consistently than Likert, and conclusions change once intensity is measured better. Later version in PSRM 13(2) — [working paper](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:tse:iastwp:126569), [TSE listing](https://www.tse-fr.eu/publications/who-cares-measuring-preference-intensity-polarized-environment).
- The same authors argue respondents inflate views on Likert items, and that QV can reduce this — [TSE WP](https://publications.ut-capitole.fr/44293/1/wp_tse_1297.pdf).
- Quarfoot et al. tested QV as a market-research tool — [Public Choice](https://www.sci.sdsu.edu/crmse/msed/papers/quarfoot2.pdf). **Correction to the brief:** the retrieved sources do not show a Quarfoot-Weyl Science 2017 paper. The paper found is in Public Choice.

### Inferences
- A forced budget (for example ten credits across a small topic set) fights the "everything matters" inflation on Likert importance ratings, and yields the person's issue public.
- Weighted matching should use importance: two people aligned on what each cares most about differ from two aligned on trivia. Importance is also the best candidate for finding cross-cluster bridges on shared priorities.
- Quadratic voting needs instruction and mental effort, so it should be optional and kept short.

### Gaps
- Sample sizes and effects for QV studies were not retrieved. No direct test of importance as a reducer of measurement error was found.

## Clusters versus continuous space: are types real?

### Takeaway
Treat continuous positions as the measurement and clusters as a labeled convenience. The strongest evidence from psychology is that differences are latently continuous, and politics has no clear taxometric evidence the other way. Types are useful summaries, not discovered kinds.

### Cited Findings
- Across 317 taxometric findings from 183 articles, dimensional findings outnumbered taxonic ones about 5 to 1 — [Haslam et al. 2020](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/dimensions-over-categories-a-meta-analysis-of-taxometric-research/).
- No domain showed aggregate taxonic support — [Haslam summary](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1450962-dimensions-over-categories--a-meta-analysis-of-taxometric-research).
- Questionnaire-based tests depend heavily on the chosen symptoms and samples — [review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3740760).
- Gerlach et al. found at least four personality types in more than 1.5 million people, as dense regions among many spurious solutions — [Nature Human Behaviour record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nathum/v2y2018i10d10.1038_s41562-018-0419-z.html).
- Critics concurred on four meaningful types but disputed robustness and exhaustiveness; the authors share the concern about premature use in assessment — [reply](https://amaral.northwestern.edu/publications/reply-four-personality-types-may-be-neither-robust-nor-exhaustive/). A co-author of the critique holds that personality is a continuum — [idw](https://idw-online.de/en/news724322).
- In politics, type counts vary with method: Feldman and Johnston 6, Baldassarri and Goldberg 3, Pew 9, Hidden Tribes 7 (sources above).

### Inferences
- Those differing counts come from different items, samples and methods, and that is itself evidence that the number is not a fact of nature.
- The Gerlach pattern suggests the honest form: **soft membership** with probabilities, dense regions named, and many people in none. A person gets a position, nearest neighborhoods with distances, and a confidence level.
- Report classification uncertainty, because people near boundaries will flip between sessions. This matters most at the MOVE layer, where cluster assignment feeds scoring.
- The existing report flags that a fixed partition is structurally weak for scoring; the measurement literature gives an independent reason for the same caution.

### Gaps
- No taxometric or latent-class-vs-factor comparison of political ideology was found. The direct test, with factor mixture models on a Dialogos sample, remains to be done. Latent profile over-extraction evidence was not retrieved.

## Practical recommendation: blueprint for 20, 40 and 80 items

### Takeaway
Build one calibrated item bank, then serve it at three depths. Everything here is my proposal, derived from the evidence above and not validated. The answer to Benjamin's question is yes, conditionally: a fingerprint is buildable and useful for matching, if it is framed as a measured, uncertain, changeable profile and not as an identity.

### Cited Findings
- Precedents for size: 30 items for nine types — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/06/10/beyond-red-vs-blue-the-political-typology/); about 30 statements in VAAs — [Gemenis](https://ecpr.eu/Events/Event/PaperDetails/31348); 36 items for six foundations — [MFQ-2 summary](https://www.psypost.org/decoding-morality-across-cultures-insights-from-a-mega-study-of-moral-foundations-theory/); 57 items for 19 values — [PVQ-RR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9131418).

### Inferences

**Shared design.**
- Bank of about 300 to 500 items, seeded from quote-anchored propositions, each tagged by layer and by source landmark.
- Answers per the scale above, item profile stored raw, axes treated as derived and versioned.
- Each item has both-sided wording review by people from the described clusters. An item is retained only if both sides accept it as a fair statement. This ties item writing directly to meta-consensus.

| | **20 items: "Glimpse"** (about 3 min) | **40 items: "Profile"** (about 7 min) | **80 items: "Fingerprint"** (about 15 min, in sessions) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Coarse placement and an invitation | Reportable person-level scores | Item-level matching and research-grade map linking |
| Anchors | 8 fixed items, the same for everyone | 12 fixed | 16 fixed |
| Adaptive | 10 items, targeting the 2 strongest axes | 22 items across 3 to 4 axes | 50 items across 5 to 6 axes plus domain modules |
| Random exploration | 2 | 3 | 8, for bank calibration and new-axis discovery |
| Retest and quality | 1 repeat, within the 20 | 2 repeats, 1 attention item | 4 repeats, 2 attention items |
| Extras | One-question importance pick | 10-credit budget across 6 topics | Budget plus trade-off (conjoint/MaxDiff) module; open "what's missing" question feeding the bank |
| Layer mix | Mostly values, a few live issues | Values, worldview, policy | All layers including epistemic and fast-moving contested claims |
| Output | Nearest 2 neighborhoods, wide uncertainty, no type label | Axis scores with intervals, soft memberships | Full profile, "fit" statement, bridge opportunities |
| Expected axis reliability (illustrative, from above) | Rough at best | About .8 on 2 to 3 axes | Up to about .8 to .9 on 5 to 6 axes |

The item counts in this table are starting hypotheses. The counts are consistent with the about-10-items-per-axis floor, except for the 20-item tier, which is deliberately under it and must say so.

**Validation sequence.**
1. Item-level cognitive interviews across clusters, including the "wrong question" option.
2. Calibration sample with random item exposure. Size is unsourced here; multidimensional IRT will need many responses per item, so plan a simulation first.
3. Dimensionality check: compare factor, mixture and latent class fits, with pre-registered holdout.
4. DIF across clusters and demographics; drop or split non-invariant items.
5. Test-retest at 2 weeks, 3 months and 12 months, reporting item-level and profile-level stability and how often people change neighborhoods.
6. Known-groups test: do landmarks' self-answered or transcript-coded responses land where the map predicts? Then let them dispute it.
7. Predictive test: does the fingerprint predict a person's unseen answers better than party ID and better than demographics?
8. Meaningful-use test: do people accept their description ("that's me"), with "that's not me" collected as a label?
9. Random-sample audit, since opt-in samples will over-represent the engaged.

**Link to memetic space.** Landmarks carry stances on the same proposition IDs, so people and landmarks share one item space. This is a joint scaling problem rather than a text-similarity match. The bridge items must be ones the landmark actually addressed, with quote anchors, and silence must not count as disagreement.

**What it can reveal.** Coarse position, a person's specific mix of views, priorities, where they feel torn, what frames they refuse, and how structured they are.

**What it cannot reveal.** Deep identity, sincerity, future behavior, why someone holds a view, or stable type. It reads a sample of current considerations under one wording. Nothing here shows it can predict how someone would move in dialogue.

**Risks.**
- A sensitive profile of beliefs is a privacy liability; default to private and on-device where possible, and do not publish individual results.
- Do not show the map of quizzes as a representation of the public, since the sample is self-selected.
- Showing people a label may entrench it. Identity-affirming feedback is a plausible backfire, and I found no direct study, so A/B test this and keep the "type name" out of early versions.
- Gaming: fingerprint answers must not feed reputation directly.

### Gaps
- No published sample-size rule was retrieved for multidimensional calibration. All time estimates, item splits and the reliability row are unvalidated proposals.
- No precedent was found for mapping survey respondents and transcript-coded public figures into one fingerprint space, which is the riskiest step. Joint scaling of votes and text has precedent per the existing report, but person-level validity here is untested.
- Whether Dialogos should do this at all before Slice 1 finishes is a decision for Benjamin, and it reopens D16.
