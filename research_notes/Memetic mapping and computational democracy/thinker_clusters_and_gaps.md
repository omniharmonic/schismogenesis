## 1. For each cluster: lineage, what binds it, what splits it, self-description, and whether the label would be accepted

### Takeaway
Most clusters have a real shared ancestry, but three are mainly coalitions of convenience: Builders, Power realists and Human-first critics. The labels most likely to be rejected are "Power realists" (for Sacks) and "Builders" (for Amodei). The strongest lineage link the map hides is that "AI risk rationalists", "Long-term stewards" and Hanson come from overlapping transhumanist, rationalist and EA roots.

### Cited Findings

#### Shared root: the Extropian, rationalist and EA stem
- The 1990s Extropian list included Bostrom, Drexler, Hanson, Kurzweil, Minsky, Szabo, Yudkowsky and Hal Finney. Max More wrote *The Principles of Extropy* and co-founded the Extropy Institute, which closed in 2006. — [Extropianism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extropianism)
- Sources cited on that page argue that Extropian thinkers helped lay the groundwork for cryptocurrency. They also link Thiel's relationship with Yudkowsky to the modern AI field. — [Extropianism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extropianism)

#### AI risk rationalists (Kokotajlo, Yudkowsky, Scott Alexander)
- **Roots:** Overcoming Bias (2006); Yudkowsky's Sequences (2006–2009, collected as *Rationality: From AI to Zombies*); LessWrong (2009); *Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality*; Slate Star Codex (2013), then Astral Codex Ten (2021). — [Rationalist community (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community)
- **Institutions and funders:** CFAR and MIRI. Named donors include Musk, Thiel (reportedly over $1 million to MIRI), Buterin, Moskovitz and Tallinn. — [Rationalist community (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community)
- **Self-description:** "rationalist", with "rationalist-adjacent", "post-rationalist" and "EA-adjacent" for people who have drifted. The stated aim is epistemic and instrumental rationality. — [Rationalist community (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community)
- **Fractures:** post-rationalists (TPOT) call the community dogmatic. The Zizians are a violent splinter, and some rationalists do not identify as EAs. — [Rationalist community (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community)
- **A fracture visible in the sample's titles:** Alexander's "Why I Am Not (As Much Of) A Doomer (As Some People)" marks distance from Yudkowsky. — [Astral Codex Ten](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/why-i-am-not-as-much-of-a-doomer)
- **Key history:** Tom Chivers's *The Rationalist's Guide to the Galaxy* (2019), also cited as *The AI Does Not Hate You*. — [Rationalist community (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalist_community), [Effective altruism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_altruism)

#### Long-term stewards (Tegmark, Buterin, MacAskill)
- **EA roots:** Singer's "Famine, Affluence, and Morality" (1972), Ord, MacAskill, GiveWell (2007), Giving What We Can and 80,000 Hours. Open Philanthropy was backed by Moskovitz and Tuna; the FTX Future Fund collapsed in 2022. — [Effective altruism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_altruism)
- **Internal split in EA:** global health, longtermism and animal welfare are diverging priorities. — [Effective altruism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_altruism)
- **EA critics:** Srinivasan, Lichtenberg and Snow (neglect of structural change), Wenar (FTX), Gebru and Torres (TESCREAL), and *Time* (misconduct reporting, 2023). — [Effective altruism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_altruism)
- **FLI link:** founded in 2014 by Tegmark, Tallinn, Krakovna, Chita-Tegmark and Aguirre. Musk gave $10 million in 2015. A $25 million Buterin-funded grant programme was announced in 2021. — [Future of Life Institute (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_Life_Institute)
- **Buterin does not call himself a steward.** The d in d/acc can mean defense, decentralization, democracy or differential. Buterin calls d/acc "a subspecies of e/acc", is wary of top-down licensing, and criticises pause messaging that gives builders no alternative. — [Buterin, "My techno-optimism"](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/techno_optimism.html)

#### Builders (Altman, Amodei, Andreessen, Musk, Verdon, Vance)
- **Andreessen's manifesto** lists Nietzsche, Hayek, Friedman, Simon and Sowell among its patron saints, with Nick Land, @BasedBeffJezos and @bayeslord. It credits Land with "techno-capital machine". Its enemies include "existential risk", "trust and safety", "tech ethics", the Precautionary Principle and de-growth. — [Techno-Optimist Manifesto](https://a16z.com/the-techno-optimist-manifesto/)
- **e/acc origins:** a May 2022 newsletter by four pseudonymous accounts (@BasedBeffJezos, @bayeslord, @zestular, @creatine_cycle), with thermodynamic ideas drawn from Jeremy England. Forbes disclosed Verdon's identity in December 2023. Critic Thomas Murphy called e/acc "Nick Land diluted for LinkedIn". — [Effective accelerationism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effective_accelerationism)
- **Dispute over e/acc's lineage:** critics stress Land. The Latecomer history notes that e/acc's founding documents do not mention Marx or French philosophers, and describes it as rebranded libertarianism. I saw this only via a search summary. — [Latecomer mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://latecomermag.com/article/a-brief-history-of-accelerationism)
- **Amodei breaks with this group's framing.** Amodei rejects the "doomer" label, warns against leaders casting themselves as "a prophet leading their people to salvation", dislikes the term "AGI", prefers "powerful AI", and calls risk work "the only thing standing between us and" a positive future. — [Machines of Loving Grace](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)
- **Vance:** the seed places Vance in `national-conservatives` as a bridger, the same broad family as Hawley's NatCon speech. — `seed/SEED-v4.json`; [Hawley, NatCon remarks](https://www.dailysignal.com/2025/09/04/hawley-ai-threatens-the-working-man/)

#### Human-first critics (Bender, Gebru, Hao, Hawley)
- **Bender and Hanna:** AI hype is a "mask for Big Tech's drive for profit". The book answers takeover and replacement claims with "no", "they wish", "LOL". — [The AI Con](https://thecon.ai/)
- **Gebru and Torres:** TESCREAL names an "interconnected and overlapping" bundle descended from 20th-century eugenics. — [TESCREAL (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL)
- **Hao:** OpenAI as a colonial-style empire, based on reporting on data workers in Colombia, the Philippines, Venezuela and Kenya. In November 2025 Hao acknowledged a 1,000-fold water figure error in the book. — [Empire of AI (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_AI)
- **Hawley:** roots the case in the *Arbella* covenant, Winthrop, Lincoln's Homestead Act, Gompers, and Pope Leo's encyclical. Rejects UBI as a "hollow answer" and opposes both "technocratic experts" and oligarchs. — [First Things](https://firstthings.com/the-american-covenants-answer-to-ai/)
- **Hawley on limits:** "We are limited creatures and there's a dignity in that." — [Compact](https://www.compactmag.com/article/josh-hawley-we-must-bend-ai-to-serve-the-good/)

#### Power realists (Yarvin, Sacks, Land)
- **Neoreaction's sources:** Hoppe, Mises, Rothbard, Carlyle, Hobbes, Evola and Sailer, with admiration for Lee Kuan Yew and Deng. Key terms are "the Cathedral" and "exit" over "voice". — [Dark Enlightenment (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Enlightenment)
- **Fractures:** Bannon treats Yarvin as an enemy. Thiel says Yarvin's ideas would not "work". Andreessen says one can read Yarvin "without becoming a monarchist". Yarvin criticised DOGE. Many accelerationists distanced themselves from Land over race. — [Dark Enlightenment (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Enlightenment)
- **Scholarship named there:** Noys, Burrows, Tait, Jones, Woods, Hodrick, Yuk Hui. — [Dark Enlightenment (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Enlightenment); also [Smith and Burrows 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/173748/7/smith-burrows-2021-software-sovereignty-and-the-post-neoliberal-politics-of-exit.pdf) (title seen, findings not read).
- **Sacks has a different lineage:** co-wrote *The Diversity Myth* (1995) with Thiel, became White House AI and crypto czar, and is reported to have announced the end of that role in March 2026 while staying PCAST co-chair. — [David Sacks (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sacks)
- **Seed placement:** Sacks and Thiel are both in `tech-right`. Yarvin is in the neoreaction niche. Land is an edge member of e/acc with status pending. — `seed/SLICE-1-LANDMARKS.md`

#### Progress pragmatists (Crawford, Hanson, Cowen)
- **Roots:** Cowen and Collison's 2019 *Atlantic* article; Crawford's Roots of Progress; *Works in Progress* (Stripe Press); Institute for Progress. — [Progress studies (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_studies)
- **Critics:** focus on total growth over inequality, and methodological doubts about the claim that disruptive science is declining. — [Progress studies (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_studies)
- **Abundance is a nearby but distinct branch:** Klein and Thompson (March 2025), with left critics Moyn, Levitz, Teachout, Kazis and Zelizer. — [Abundance (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abundance_(Klein_and_Thompson_book))
- **Hanson is an odd fit:** GMU economist, former FHI research associate, cryonics supporter, inventor of LMSR, proposer of futarchy, author of *The Age of Em*. — [Robin Hanson (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hanson)
- **Self-term:** Crawford's own word is "solutionism". — [Roots of Progress](https://newsletter.rootsofprogress.org/p/a-plea-for-solutionism-on-ai-safety)

#### Pluralists (Tang, Weyl)
- *Plurality* was written openly on GitHub by Weyl, Tang and "the Plurality Community", and positions itself between "techno-libertarianism and centralized AI governance". — [plurality.net](https://www.plurality.net/)
- Weyl calls *The Sovereign Individual* a political manifesto dressed as prophecy, and urges people not to take funding from its adherents. — [Sovereign Nonsense](https://www.radicalxchange.org/updates/blog/sovereign-nonsense/)
- Self-terms are explicit: "Why I Am a Pluralist", "Why I Am Not A Technocrat". — [RadicalxChange](https://www.radicalxchange.org/updates/blog/why-i-am-a-pluralist/), [RadicalxChange](https://www.radicalxchange.org/updates/blog/2019-08-19-bv61r6/)

#### Exit founders (Balaji Srinivasan, Thiel)
- **Thiel (2009):** "no longer believe[s] that freedom and democracy are compatible". Names cyberspace, outer space and seasteading as escapes. — [Cato Unbound](https://www.cato-unbound.org/2009/04/13/peter-thiel/education-libertarian/)
- Thiel wrote the preface to the current edition of *The Sovereign Individual*, per Weyl. — [Sovereign Nonsense](https://www.radicalxchange.org/updates/blog/sovereign-nonsense/)

## 2. What existing scholarship says about these groups, including empirical maps of AI discourse

### Takeaway
The scholarship is strong on single groups but weak on the whole field. No peer-reviewed study I reached maps the full AI worldview space by person. The most useful empirical result is that mass Twitter AI talk in January 2023 sorted by use (art, crypto, data science), not by safety versus ethics. The Atlas camps are therefore an elite structure, not a public one.

### Cited Findings

#### TESCREAL and its critics
- Transhumanism, Extropianism, Singularitarianism, Cosmism, Rationalism, Effective Altruism and Longtermism; proposed 2023, published in *First Monday* April 2024. — [TESCREAL (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL); [article](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13636/11599)
- **Ozy Brennan:** the label makes distinct views monolithic, treats thought experiments as policy, and the real dispute is over which utopia. — [Asterisk](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/06/the-tescreal-bungle)
- **Sennesh and Hughes:** a left conspiracy theory joining mutually exclusive tenets. **Lazar:** "highly polemical". **Habryka:** has never met a cosmist. **McLauchlan:** EAs unfairly cast as a eugenics plot. **Pethokoukis:** defends the billionaires' futurism. — [TESCREAL (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TESCREAL)

#### AI safety as an epistemic culture
- Ahmed, Jaźwińska, Ahlawat, Winecoff and Wang (*First Monday* 29:4, April 2024) argue that EA, longtermism and x-risk communities formed one "AI safety epistemic community". Its four sites are forums and career advising, forecasting, research, and prizes. — [First Monday](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/view/13626)
- Ahmed's earlier post asks whether this could become an epistemic monoculture. — [Crooked Timber](https://crookedtimber.org/2023/11/16/from-algorithmic-monoculture-to-epistemic-monoculture-understanding-the-rise-of-ai-safety)

#### Ethics versus safety
- Cave and Ó hÉigeartaigh argue the near-term and long-term split is a mistake, since near-term work shapes long-term outcomes. — [Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/293033)
- Baum (*AI & Society* 2018) proposes that the more useful line is "societalist" versus "intellectualist", which cuts across near-term and long-term. — [GCRI](https://gcri.org/publications/research/reconciliation-near-long-term-ai/)
- Prunkl and Whittlestone replace near/long with four dimensions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.04335)
- Kasirzadeh's "accumulative" x-risk (gradual erosion of institutions and security leading to a tipping point) can reconcile seemingly incompatible positions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07836)

#### Empirical maps
- **Kaiser and Green (*First Monday* 31:4, April 2026):** all tweets mentioning AI in January 2023 (N=4,732,859). Communities in the retweet network: Engineering & Business, three AI Art groups, Crypto, Tech, Data Science, Web 3. They found "no community that discussed AI specifically", little presence of mass media, and that AI art was among the most polarized. — [First Monday PDF](https://firstmonday.org/ojs/index.php/fm/article/download/14436/12423)
- **Brooks (EA Forum, November 2025, not peer reviewed):** about 740 accounts, and about 400k tweets since March 2023, clustered by AI summaries of views into five groups: Pragmatic Safety Establishment, High P(doom)ers, Frontier Capability Maximizers, Open Source Democratizers, Paradigm Skeptics. — [EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/JxiEyRtaApvqAZ2cQ/ai-discourse-analyzed-we-looked-at-essays-twitter-bluesky)
- **Pew (April 2025):** experts versus public on positive US impact over 20 years, 56% versus 17%. Excited rather than concerned, 47% versus 11%. Both want more control (57% and 55%), and among experts 63% of men versus 36% of women expect positive impact. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/03/how-the-us-public-and-ai-experts-view-artificial-intelligence/)

### Inferences
- Brooks's five groups are the closest outside benchmark for the Atlas. Pragmatic Safety and Frontier Maximizers would likely divide Builders, and Open Source Democratizers has no Atlas school at all. The mapping is my inference.
- Baum's societalist line may explain the Atlas finding that safety and critics share most layers. That is worth testing as an axis.
- TESCREAL is itself a claim about clusters. The Atlas can test it, since the claim implies shared roots. The critics' prediction is that those holdings will not co-occur.
- The Kaiser and Green result implies a "publics" layer is needed alongside elite thinkers. The January 2023 date predates much of the current fight.

### Gaps
- Stix and Maas's "incompletely theorized agreement" paper could not be read (Springer redirect), so it is not summarised.
- Cave and Dihal's "Hopes and fears for intelligent machines" and "The Whiteness of AI" could not be opened. Their work on AI narratives is **not verified here**.
- I found no peer-reviewed person-level network map of safety, ethics and acceleration. This may be a search limit and not a real absence.
- The Midas Project's claim about coordinated anti-"doomer" accounts surfaced only as a snippet from an advocacy source and was not assessed. — [X article](https://x.com/TheMidasProj/article/2103577207700365719)
- No peer-reviewed e/acc study was found. Only one 2024 CRC chapter was named, unread.
- Histories of EA beyond Wikipedia, and studies of the postliberal right, were not reached.

## 5. Which wider US typologies are strongest, and how do they compare with this approach?

### Takeaway
Pew is strongest for population validity, Hidden Tribes for psychological drivers, and American Nations for place, though it has the weakest empirical base among those read. None does what the Atlas does, which is to tie a position to quotable claims in layers. The Atlas's weakness is the reverse: it has no population behind it.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences

- Moral Foundations can tag the values layer, but should not be treated as causal, given the longitudinal critique.
- Woodard could add a place dimension to the seed, but only as a hypothesis until tested.
- The Atlas's strongest claim over these is that a person can check a sentence attributed to them. No typology here offers that.
