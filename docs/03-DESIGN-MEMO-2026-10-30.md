# Dialogos Arena — Design Dialogue Memo

*Benjamin Life (@omniharmonic) in thought partnership with Claude · October 30, 2026 · Supersedes parts of the research synthesis where noted*

This memo captures the decisions reached in our first-principles design conversation after the research swarm. It builds on the research synthesis filed in Parachute under `vault/research/dialogos-arena/`.

## 1. Strategic posture

- The aim is a deeply viral, **doubly mimetic** product: people join because others are on the map, and the map itself is a picture of mimesis (who resembles whom). It should appeal even to Thiel, Andreessen and prediction-market audiences through a sophisticated grasp of mimetic theory, while carrying a thesis they lack: the dialectic is an inherent part of participation in the polis.
- The danger of the easy viral loop: "find your tribe and see your enemies" hardens clusters. Every design choice must point the mimetic energy toward understanding instead.
- The feed algorithm runs in reverse. Where engagement algorithms produce **reciprocal narrowing**, the Arena aims at **reciprocal opening** (John Vervaeke's term; to be researched): rank by what widens, not what holds you.
- Avoid navel-gazing. The process must produce visible, concrete outputs, not general facilitation with no deliverable.

## 2. The gradient

**Decided:** the gradient the system ascends is *how accurately each cluster can describe every other cluster, in terms the described cluster would sign.* This mirrors anti-debate, where a ratified steelman is a precondition for the next phase. It cannot be faked by blandness and requires no one to change their mind. Success is meta-consensus, not convergence.

## 3. Cross-cluster notes

Community-Notes-style notes point from one cluster to another, written by someone in cluster A about cluster B. Lineage: Jamie Joyce and the Society Library, Audrey Tang and vTaiwan, Community Notes.

Each note carries two signals:

- **Fidelity:** members of B confirm "that's us, I'd sign that." Only B can grant it.
- **Portability:** members of A and third clusters find it clarifying (cross-viewpoint agreement, as in Community Notes).

The more clusters agree on a shared description of a given cluster's perspective, the further up the gradient we are. When B splits on whether a note describes them, the split reveals a sub-cluster and sharpens the map. Notes describe what B believes and why; they carry no judgments about B, which keeps them signable.

## 4. Synthesis and novelty

- Notes are static descriptions; a synthesis is dynamic and becomes a new thesis. Benjamin wants that dialectical churn in the incentive.
- **Decided:** novelty is not rewarded at proposal. LLMs make plausible "third perspectives" nearly free, and most are midpoints dressed as transcendence.
- A synthesis may be proposed only after ratified steelmen exist in both directions.
- Syntheses are rewarded **retroactively by lineage**: uptake by both parent clusters, gathering its own adherents, appearing on the map as a new region tracing to both parents. Novelty is proven by descendants, not claimed by authors.
- A clean, mutually understood disagreement is a success, never a failure. Otherwise people manufacture fake syntheses.
- Often the best fruit of mutual steelmanning is not a third answer but **a better question** both sides keep asking. This is harder to fake and observable through uptake.

## 5. Scenius: evolution within clusters

- Most new ideas are born inside a scene. A scene's internal dialogue is collective intelligence, and belonging is earned by contributing to it.
- Risk: rewarding within-group contribution can fund purity spirals (group polarization), where status comes from attacking the outgroup.
- Distinguish **deepening** (the scene's own thesis gets richer: new questions, internal disagreements worked through, ideas the group takes up) from **hardening** (status earned against another cluster). Hardening is detectable because it is defined against an outgroup.
- One currency for both: **lineage**. An idea taken up within its own scene is scenius; an idea taken up across scenes is synthesis.

## 6. Engine and magnet

- **Engine:** the incentive gradient (ratified descriptions, lineage rewards).
- **Magnet:** the living map itself, a place to return daily and watch culture think. Self-placement is a one-time curiosity; a live map of the collective conversation is a habit.
- The core object of the live map is the **live thread**: an open question a scene is working through, which moves, splits and leaks across boundaries. The deepest unit may be the question, not the claim. The map becomes a genealogy of questions.
- **Axes:** the data can hold many dimensions; humans see two or three. Each live question chooses the axes it actually splits people along, and the map reorients around the thread you follow.
- **Guard against spectatorship:** the people on thresholds, who translate between scenes, are the protagonists. The map constantly invites you to become one.
- Rhyme noted with Forrest Landry's Ephemeral Group Process (inquiry-based, collectively developed questions, small groups at scale, an archive of community questions; presented at The Stoa in 2020). Inspiration only: it never delivered a full stack with concrete outputs.

## 7. How the map is built (decided architecture)

### 7.1 Why not the social graph as the core

Following is attention, not agreement: many people follow figures they oppose because those figures matter culturally. The follow graph maps attention, not belief. X's API also makes large follower pulls expensive and restricts political inference, and archive uploads are too slow (a day or more) to be the front door. The idea map must not depend on X's terms.

### 7.2 Landmark-content map

1. **Seed landmarks.** Curate a US-focused set of public voices, at least two or three per niche, covering as many semi-mainstream niches as possible (e.g., looksmaxxing, neoliberals, fiscal conservatives). Start from Limberg & Barnes's tribes, Kevin Owocki's 58 ontologies, and our own brainstorm; let data reveal missed clusters and name them afterward. Placing public intellectuals from their public output is acceptable: it describes the clustering of a worldview, not a political categorization. Landmarks get a way to dispute or annotate their placement.
2. **Multi-platform ingestion of public output:** YouTube transcripts, podcasts, Substacks, posts. Bounded scope: a few hundred voices, not the whole internet.
3. **Representative sampling by niche, not volume.** Every scene gets a fair share whether loud or quiet.
4. **Edges from content:** who quotes whom, who appears on whose show, who argues with whom by name. Cross-cluster encounters are far more meaningful than follows.
5. **Bluesky** remains a useful low-friction channel but is demographically skewed, so it cannot carry the map alone.

### 7.3 Extraction pipeline (where fidelity lives or dies)

1. **Quote-anchored extraction.** A model proposes claims from each transcript; each must point to the exact supporting words, or it is discarded. This guards against AI-invented beliefs (Ontography's weakness).
2. **Neutral propositions.** Each claim is rewritten speaker-independently (e.g., "Immigration levels should be cut sharply"), and identical propositions from different people merge into one **shared proposition bank** (Topology Live's design).
3. **Layer tagging with Jev:** what is real, how we know, what matters, what is empirically true, what to do (the anti-debate taxonomy).
4. **Statement selection** for people to react to: statements that split clusters cleanly; statements that divide a scene internally (its live threads); statements that surprisingly unite distant clusters (the threshold moments).
5. **Every statement links back to its source clip** so anyone can verify it.

### 7.4 Ideas at scale, cheaply

- Weekly, a stronger model reads a sample of landmark output and proposes the questions people are actually contesting now: the live question battery comes from the culture.
- Jev labels cheaply at scale against that battery.
- No surveys. People only correct mistakes ("that's not me"), and each correction becomes a labeled example.

### 7.5 Placing individuals: statement reactions

- Individuals place themselves, platform-independently, by reacting to real statements from across the map: agree, disagree, pass (as in Polis). Gamified by guessing who said it before the reveal.
- This placement also defines **cluster membership for ratifying notes**: membership comes from what you endorse, not whom you follow, which makes ratification much harder to game.

## 8. The first sixty seconds

1. You react to a handful of statements and appear as a dot on a terrain that already exists.
2. You see your three nearest landmarks.
3. **The threshold reveal:** which clusters you sit between. Most people assume they're in one camp; seeing themselves straddle two is the surprise.
4. **The twist card:** the landmark farthest from you whom you most resemble on one question. "Wait, I agree with them about that?" This is the shareable moment.
5. The final screen is an invitation, not a score: describe the cluster farthest from you, and see if they'll sign it.

The status prize shared socially should be "the other side signed my description of them": understanding, not placement.

## 9. Time: the four-dimensional map

- Everything is timestamped from day one. Propositions record first appearance and speaker; stances are readings at a moment.
- Clusters are tracked as **lineages** across snapshots: births, splits, merges, fadings (e.g., Occupy, BLM, the America First split).
- Threads have a life cycle: emerge, spread within a scene, fork, cross into another scene, go quiet or get absorbed. Crossing is the moment that matters most.
- **Backfill:** YouTube and podcast archives allow real historical arcs on day one.
- **Guard:** keep a stable core panel of landmark voices, with newcomers clearly marked, so sampling changes don't masquerade as cultural movement.

### Nested time horizons

| Horizon | Work | Product |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily — the pulse | Cheap Jev tagging of new output against existing questions; flag spikes and events (e.g., a major cross-cluster interview) | A daily pulse, like Kevin's "Attention Times" |
| Weekly — the weather | Re-run clustering; update thread life cycles; propose new live questions | A weekly culture weather report |
| Monthly — the deep pass | Fresh proposition extraction; re-fit axes; trace cluster lineages; audit against user corrections | A monthly state-of-the-discourse report for journalists and researchers |

## 11. Addendum: event maps and claim seeding (same session, later)

The existing Topology Live / anti-debate extraction becomes the main seeding engine for the map: claims come from dialogue, not tweets.

### 11.1 Event maps

- Input: one cross-cluster encounter (interview, debate, podcast, panel).
- Pipeline: transcript split by speaker → quote-anchored propositions per speaker → matched to the shared proposition bank.
- The map shows: where each speaker starts on the terrain; where they met (propositions both affirmed); where they diverged and at which layer (facts, values, ontology — Dialectical Topology's lens on the shared map); missed openings where a steelman would have changed the conversation.
- **The ripple:** over following days, the daily pulse tracks whether the event's ideas crossed into other scenes or hardened each side.
- **Virality through speed:** publish the map of a major crossover moment within a day, riding the news cycle.
- **Caution on the first public pick:** choose a surprising but less incendiary first event; save the most charged ones (e.g., Nick Fuentes) until the method has earned trust, to avoid a launch defined by platforming accusations.

### 11.2 Seeding the map with dialogue extraction (Benjamin's insight)

- Tweets and posts are a poor way to capture the depth of a mimetic structure. Dialogue forces people to say what they believe, why, and at which layer.
- **Clarification:** the extraction does not require anti-debate-structured events. It maps claims from any debate, dialogue, interview or monologue.
- **Decided:** scrape YouTube (and podcast) transcripts for the full landmark set (\~500 figureheads) and generate quote-anchored claims from them. This is the baseline content of the map.
- Each cross-cluster encounter is an **edge** between two clusters. Goal: cover the terrain so any cluster can be reached from any other through a chain of encounters. Some clusters may have no overlap at all; pairwise hops still let us map the terrain conceptually.
- **Gap-filling:** look for cluster pairs that have never met; those become invitations for the Arena to host its own anti-debates, chosen to fill the biggest holes. This is both research method and content engine.

### 11.3 Caveats

- People who talk across lines are usually bridgers, not cluster cores. Event content shows clusters at their most open, so landmarks' everyday output remains the baseline.
- Topology Live's live speaker attribution was not fully validated; seed from recorded content first.
